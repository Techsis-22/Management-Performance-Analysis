

# Management Performance Analysis Dashboard | Power BI Business Intelligence Project

## 1. Project Overview

Businesses generate large volumes of data through their daily operations. However, having data is not enough. The real value lies in transforming that data into meaningful insights that support informed business decisions.

For this project, I developed an interactive Management Performance Dashboard in Microsoft Power BI to provide a consolidated view of business performance across key operational metrics.

The dashboard examines total revenue, reported profit, customer return rates, average discounts, sales performance across different markets, product category contributions, and monthly revenue trends.

The objective was to transform raw business data into a clear, interactive, and visually accessible report that enables management to identify business strengths, detect potential areas of concern, and make data-driven decisions.

Rather than looking at individual figures in isolation, the dashboard brings different performance indicators together to tell a broader story about the business.

## 2. Business Problem

Management teams need timely and reliable information to understand whether a business is performing as expected. When sales, profit, discounts, and returns are examined separately, identifying relationships between these metrics can become difficult.

For this project, I approached the analysis with the following business questions:

- How much revenue and profit is the business generating?
- Which markets contribute the most to reported profit?
- Which product categories generate the highest revenue?
- How has revenue changed over the months?
- Which sales channels record the highest return rates?
- What is the average discount offered to customers?
- What areas should management investigate to improve overall performance?

The dashboard was designed to answer these questions through a combination of KPI cards, summary tables, a column chart, and a monthly trend chart.

The relationship between revenue and profit is particularly important. A business may generate substantial sales without necessarily achieving the same level of profitability. Monitoring both metrics helps management evaluate financial performance more comprehensively.

## 4. Key Findings and Business Insights

### A. Revenue and Profitability

The business generated total revenue of **₦46.68 million** and reported profit of **₦14.59 million** during the reporting period.

Assuming revenue and reported profit are calculated on a consistent basis, reported profit represents approximately 31.25% of revenue. This provides an initial indication of profitability, although a formal profit margin calculation should confirm the underlying definitions and included costs.

**Business implication:** Management should continue monitoring revenue alongside profitability to determine whether sales growth translates into improved financial performance.

Further analysis could examine operating expenses, product-level margins, and the effect of discounts on profitability.

### B. Profit Performance Across Markets

The market analysis shows differences in reported profit across five locations.

| Market        | Reported Profit |
| ------------- | --------------- |
| Lagos         | ₦4,579,900      |
| Kano          | ₦3,243,600      |
| Ibadan        | ₦2,730,325      |
| Port Harcourt | ₦2,338,500      |
| Abuja         | ₦1,698,100      |

**Key insight:** Lagos recorded the highest reported profit, followed by Kano and Ibadan. Abuja recorded the lowest reported profit among the five markets displayed.

Lagos generated approximately 31.4% of the total reported profit, making it the strongest market in this comparison.

**Business implication:** Management could investigate the factors contributing to Lagos's performance, including sales volume, customer demand, product mix, pricing, and distribution efficiency.

The lower reported profit in Abuja also presents an opportunity for further investigation. However, lower profit does not automatically indicate poor performance. Management should compare revenue, operating costs, market size, and sales activity before deciding whether corrective action is necessary.

### C. Revenue Contribution by Product Category

The product category analysis reveals that revenue is concentrated in three categories.

| Product Category | Reported Revenue | Approximate Share of Revenue |
| ---------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Electronics      | ₦29,346,500      | 62.9%                        |
| Home             | ₦13,206,300      | 28.3%                        |
| Accessories      | ₦4,126,925       | 8.8%                         |
| **Total**        | **₦46,679,725**  | **100%**                     |

**Key insight:** Electronics is the leading revenue contributor, accounting for approximately 62.9% of total reported revenue. The Home category contributes about 28.3%, while Accessories contributes the remaining 8.8%.

This suggests that the business relies heavily on Electronics for revenue generation.

**Business implication:** Management should assess whether the Electronics category can sustain its contribution while exploring opportunities to grow revenue from Home and Accessories.

Possible actions include reviewing customer purchasing patterns, evaluating product availability, examining cross-selling opportunities, and identifying products with strong demand but low visibility.

The relatively smaller Accessories contribution should not automatically be interpreted as poor performance. Its profitability, sales volume, and strategic role in complementary purchases should also be examined.

### D. Monthly Revenue Trend

The monthly revenue analysis highlights fluctuations in sales between January and August.

| Month    | Reported Revenue |
| -------- | ---------------- |
| January  | ₦6,722,250       |
| February | ₦5,364,300       |
| March    | ₦6,298,725       |
| April    | ₦6,343,000       |
| May      | ₦7,008,500       |
| June     | ₦5,397,250       |
| July     | ₦6,378,275       |
| August   | ₦3,167,425       |

**Key insight:** May recorded the highest monthly revenue at ₦7,008,500, while August recorded the lowest at ₦3,167,425.

Revenue declined from January to February before recovering in March and April. May delivered the strongest performance, followed by a decline in June and a recovery in July.

August then recorded a substantial decrease compared with July.

**Business implication:** Management should investigate the factors behind the monthly fluctuations. Potential areas to examine include seasonal demand, stock availability, promotional activities, pricing changes, sales volume, and changes in customer purchasing behaviour.

August requires particular attention because its reported revenue was approximately 50.3% lower than July's.

However, the dashboard alone does not establish the cause of this decline. Since August is the final month displayed, management should first verify whether the month contains complete data or represents a partial reporting period before interpreting the decline as a deterioration in business performance.

### E. Return Rate Across Sales Channels

The channel analysis highlights differences in return rates across three sales channels.

| Sales Channel | Return Rate |
| ------------- | ----------- |
| In-store      | 6.4%        |
| Online        | 9.7%        |
| WhatsApp      | 11.9%       |

**Key insight:** WhatsApp recorded the highest return rate at 11.9%, followed by Online at 9.7%. In-store sales recorded the lowest return rate at 6.4%.

The WhatsApp return rate is 5.5 percentage points higher than the in-store rate. This difference makes the channel an important area for further investigation.

**Business implication:** Management could examine the customer journey across each channel to identify possible reasons for the differences.

For WhatsApp and Online sales, relevant areas include product descriptions, customer communication, order confirmation, product selection, delivery handling, and the accuracy of customer expectations.

The findings do not prove that any of these factors caused the higher return rates. Additional analysis of return reasons, order volumes, and product-level return patterns would help establish the underlying causes.

Reducing avoidable returns could improve customer satisfaction and potentially reduce reverse logistics costs.

### F. Average Discount

The dashboard reports an average discount of **8.0%**.

Discounts can be useful for attracting customers, encouraging purchases, and supporting promotional campaigns. However, excessive or poorly targeted discounts may reduce profitability without generating sufficient additional sales.

**Business implication:** Management should evaluate discounting alongside revenue, profit, product category, and sales channel.

Further analysis could determine whether higher discounts are associated with increased sales volume, improved profitability, or a reduction in profit per order.

This would help the business distinguish effective promotional activities from discounts that may be unnecessarily reducing margins.

## 5. Overall Business Story

When the findings are considered together, the dashboard highlights several important aspects of the business.

First, the business generated ₦46.68 million in revenue and ₦14.59 million in reported profit, providing a useful starting point for evaluating overall performance.

Second, Electronics is the dominant revenue category, while Lagos leads the five displayed markets in reported profit. These results identify areas of strength that management can investigate to understand the drivers of performance.

Third, revenue fluctuated throughout the reporting period, reaching its highest point in May before falling substantially in August. The final month's data coverage should be validated before conclusions are drawn about the decline.

Finally, the return-rate comparison reveals that WhatsApp and Online sales have higher reported return rates than in-store sales. This creates an opportunity to investigate channel-specific customer experiences and identify potential improvements.

Together, these findings demonstrate why management should not rely on revenue alone. A more complete view of performance considers profitability, product mix, market contribution, sales trends, customer returns, and discounting practices.

## 6. Recommendations for Management

Based on the findings, I identified the following areas for further investigation and potential action.

**1. Investigate the August revenue decline.**\
Validate the completeness of August's data and examine changes in order volume, average order value, product availability, and customer demand.

**2. Study the drivers of Lagos's profitability.**\
Compare product mix, revenue, order volume, costs, and profit margins across markets to understand what contributes to Lagos's leading position.

**3. Develop a strategy for category growth.**\
Protect the Electronics category's strong contribution while evaluating opportunities to increase Home and Accessories sales through customer insights and cross-selling.

**4. Investigate returns in WhatsApp and Online channels.**\
Analyse return reasons, affected products, order volumes, and customer feedback to identify practical ways to reduce avoidable returns.

**5. Evaluate discount effectiveness.**\
Measure how discounts affect sales volume and profitability rather than assessing promotional success through revenue alone.

**6. Introduce more detailed performance monitoring.**\
Track trends over time and compare markets, categories, and channels to identify emerging risks and opportunities.

These recommendations are intended as next steps informed by the dashboard, rather than claims that the underlying causes have already been established.

## 7. Dashboard Design and Power BI Implementation

I designed the dashboard to provide a consolidated and accessible view of business performance.

The report contains the following visual components:

- **KPI cards:** Display total revenue, total profit, return rate, and average discount.
- **Market performance table:** Compares reported profit across five locations.
- **Sales channel table and chart:** Present return-rate differences across In-store, Online, and WhatsApp channels.
- **Product category table and chart:** Compare revenue contribution across Electronics, Home, and Accessories.
- **Monthly revenue table and line chart:** Show the revenue trend from January to August.

The combination of summary metrics, detailed tables, and charts allows users to move from a high-level overview to more specific comparisons.

The green colour scheme and consistent formatting also help create a unified visual identity across the report.

The emphasis was on presenting the information in a way that supports business interpretation, rather than simply displaying as many visuals as possible.

## 9. Project Limitations and Opportunities for Improvement

Although the dashboard provides a useful overview, several enhancements could make the analysis more comprehensive.

**Profitability analysis by category and channel:** Revenue contribution alone does not show which categories or channels generate the strongest margins.

**Return reasons:** Including return reason codes would help explain why some channels have higher return rates.

**Time-period validation:** Confirming that all months represent comparable reporting periods would make trend analysis more reliable.

**Discount impact analysis:** Comparing discount levels with profit and order volume would help assess promotional effectiveness.

**Additional operational metrics:** Order volume, average order value, units sold, and fulfilment performance could provide further context.

**Interactive analysis:** If not already implemented, slicers for date, market, category, and channel could allow users to explore the report according to their specific needs.

These improvements would extend the dashboard from a descriptive performance report towards a more diagnostic business intelligence solution.
